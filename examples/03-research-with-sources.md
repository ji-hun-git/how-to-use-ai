# Example 3. Research: don't trust sources, check them

> The most dangerous thing when you hand research to AI is **plausible fake sources**.
> In the US, lawyers were sanctioned $5,000 for submitting fake case law generated by ChatGPT to a court (Mata v. Avianca, 2023). Similar cases have continued to be reported around the world since then.

---

## Three principles

1. Ask **with search turned on**. Without search, the model answers from memory, and when it has no memory of something, it invents something plausible.
2. Have it attach **a source to every claim**, and **open the links yourself** to check them.
3. Check **important numbers and quotes** against the original yourself. Even when a source link is real, the page sometimes doesn't contain that content.

> According to Stanford research (2024–2025), even specialized legal AI tools hallucinated in 17–34% of their answers. Some of these cases **cited court cases that really exist, but that don't support the claim**.

---

## Research prompt

```xml
<context>
I'm on our company's training team, and next month I'm creating "Using generative AI" training materials for new hires.
This will be reported to leadership, so the numbers and sources must be accurate.
</context>

<task>
Research findings published since 2023 on how generative AI affects workplace productivity.
</task>

<format>
| Study (authors, year, institution) | Population/size | Key findings (with numbers) | Limitations | Source URL | Peer-reviewed? |
Below the table, summarize in 3 lines "where the studies reached different conclusions."
</format>

<constraints>
- Include in the table only material you actually found and checked through search.
- Mark any figure you couldn't confirm by opening the original as [unverified].
- If you found only news articles and couldn't locate the original paper, say so.
- Don't make up anything you couldn't find; write "not found."
</constraints>
```

---

## Verification steps (don't skip these)

### 1) Ask for source verification separately

```text
For each row in the table above, check the following:
- Does the URL actually open?
- Does that page actually contain the number in the table? (Quote the sentence verbatim.)
- If you can't confirm it, mark that row [verification failed].
```

### 2) Cross-check in a different chat or with a different model

If you check within the same conversation, the model gets pulled toward its earlier answers. Ask a **new chat** or **another company's model**: "Check whether this claim is true."

### 3) What a human must look at (at minimum)

- [ ] I opened the original source for **every number** in the report
- [ ] I searched for each paper by title to confirm it actually exists (Google Scholar, etc.)
- [ ] I distinguished primary sources (papers, official reports) from secondary sources (articles, blogs)

---

## When using Deep Research features

The Deep Research features in various services automatically find dozens of sources and write a report for you. They're convenient, but they sometimes mix in sources **without regard to their quality**.

- After you get the report, run the "source verification" prompt above as-is.
- Set **source criteria** in advance, such as "Prioritize official documentation, papers, and government sources. Exclude blogs and SEO articles."
- If the conclusion seems one-sided, ask once more: "Find only the counterevidence."
