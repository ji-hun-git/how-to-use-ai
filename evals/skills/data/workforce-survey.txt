Regional Health Workforce Survey 2026 — Summary of findings (fictional example)
Prepared by the Riverbend Health Workforce Office. Published September 2026.

ABOUT THE SURVEY
The 2026 survey was conducted online between April 6 and May 15, 2026. It covered two groups working in the region's public hospitals: registered nurses and administrative staff. In total, 1,240 nurses and 310 administrative staff completed the survey. Physicians, contractors and agency staff were not included this year; a separate physician survey is planned for 2027.

KEY FINDINGS

Burnout. 58% of nurses reported symptoms of burnout "often" or "always", compared with 49% in the 2024 survey. Among administrative staff, 41% reported burnout symptoms. Administrative staff were not included in the 2024 survey, so no comparison is available for them.

Workload. Nurses reported working an average of 4.2 unpaid overtime hours per week. 63% of nurses said staffing levels on their unit were "inadequate" on most shifts. Administrative staff reported an average of 1.6 unpaid overtime hours per week.

Intention to leave. 27% of nurses said they intend to leave their current job within 12 months; of these, 52% said they would leave nursing altogether. Among administrative staff, 18% intend to leave their current job within 12 months.

Scheduling. Nurses who received their schedule at least two weeks in advance were less likely to report burnout (49%) than those who received it less than one week in advance (66%). The survey did not ask about shift length.

Pay. 71% of nurses and 54% of administrative staff rated their pay as "below" or "well below" what is fair for their role.

WHAT STAFF ASKED FOR
In open-ended answers, the most common requests from nurses were more predictable schedules, more staff per shift, and protected break time. Administrative staff most often asked for better software tools and clearer processes.

RECOMMENDATIONS
The Workforce Office recommends that hospitals:
1. Publish nurse schedules at least two weeks in advance.
2. Pilot flexible scheduling options, including self-scheduling, on at least two units per hospital in 2027.
3. Review minimum staffing levels for night shifts.
4. Replace the legacy rostering software by 2028.
The office will report on the pilots in the 2027 survey.

LIMITATIONS
Participation was voluntary, and staff experiencing burnout may have been more or less likely to respond. Response rates were 31% for nurses and 22% for administrative staff. Results are not weighted by hospital or unit.
